# 1.0.0-beta.5 — Tabs and the split view

The main area is tabbed now, and it can be split in two. The headline is the last part: you can
open one note in both panes and they are two views of *one* note, not two copies — type in either
and it appears in the other.

Full guide: **Help → Tabs and the split view**.

## What's new

### Tabs

- **Every screen opens as a tab**, in a strip across the top of the main area. A daily note shows a
  gold dot, the Planner shows `PLAN` and the name of its sub-tab, a project shows its own colour.
- **A plain click still reuses the tab you are on**, so clicking through ten notes leaves you with
  one tab, not ten. `Ctrl+click` opens a new tab when you want one.
- `Ctrl+Tab` / `Ctrl+Shift+Tab` cycle, `Ctrl+W` closes, middle-click closes, and tabs drag to
  reorder. Closing the last tab opens Today rather than leaving an empty window.
- **Back and forward are per tab now.** Each tab keeps its own history, so going back in one tab no
  longer walks you through screens you visited in another.
- `Ctrl+W` used to close the whole window — Hollow now installs its own application menu so that key
  belongs to the tab strip. Copy and paste are unaffected.

### Splitting the main area

- The split button in the top strip, or `Ctrl+Alt+\`. While split, a **Side | Stacked** control
  chooses between two panes beside each other and one above the other.
- **Drag a tab onto the other strip** to move it across; it keeps its own history when it travels.
  Or right-click it → **Move to other pane**.
- **Unsplit** merges the strips back together and drops duplicates. Closing the last tab in a pane
  closes that pane.
- **If the window is too narrow for two screens, the panes stack** — and stay stacked when you widen
  it again, rather than rearranging themselves while you drag the window.
- One pane is the focused one (its active tab is underlined in the accent colour). Rail clicks, the
  back and forward arrows, `Ctrl+N` and the right sidebar all follow it.

### One note in both panes

- Split while a note is open and you get that note twice, as **two views of one note**: typing in
  either appears in the other, undo spans both, and only one version is ever saved. The header says
  **Open in both panes · edits sync** while that is true.
- This is for writing at the bottom of a long note while reading the top of it.

### Open to the side, from anywhere

- Right-click a note in the explorer, a project in the rail, **All projects** or a Planner sub-tab
  and the menu starts with **Open**, **Open in new tab**, **Open to the side** and **Open below**.
- `Alt+click` is the shortcut for **Open to the side**. In the note explorer `Ctrl+click` and
  `Shift+click` still select several notes, so those are left alone there.
- A calendar or List chip's right-click menu opens the note a task came from, in any of those ways,
  or unschedules it. A task row in the day's list can be rescheduled to a weekday, unscheduled, or
  shown in the Calendar or List in the other pane.

### The rail's Notes and Planner buttons

- These two now choose **which list the rail shows** — your note explorer, or your projects —
  instead of changing the screen. That way you can browse your notes with the Planner open.
- `Ctrl+1` and `Ctrl+2` still change the screen.

## What to test

1. **The ordinary day should feel unchanged.** Open notes from the explorer, move between Notes and
   Planner with `Ctrl+1` / `Ctrl+2`, edit and let things autosave. One tab, no split — this is the
   path almost all use takes, and it should feel exactly like beta.4.
2. **Notes save as they always did.** This release rebuilt how a note's editor is fed, so it is the
   thing worth being suspicious of. Type in a note, wait a second, switch to another note and back.
   Then close Hollow, reopen it, and check the text is there. Anything missing is the most important
   bug you could report.
3. **One note in both panes.** Open a note, press `Ctrl+Alt+\`, and type in each pane in turn. Both
   should update as you type. Then close one pane, keep typing in the other, and reopen the note
   later to confirm what you typed is what was saved.
4. **Tabs.** `Ctrl+click` a few notes to build a strip. Cycle with `Ctrl+Tab`, close with `Ctrl+W`
   and middle-click, drag them into a different order, and close the last one (you should land on
   Today). Check `Ctrl+W` never closes the window.
5. **Back and forward per tab.** Walk one tab through three screens, open a second tab, and check
   the back arrow in the second tab does not retrace the first tab's steps.
6. **The split.** Split, drag a tab across, use **Move to other pane**, unsplit. Then narrow the
   window until the panes stack, widen it again (they should stay stacked), and press **Side**.
7. **Open to the side.** Try it from a note in the explorer, a project row, a Planner sub-tab and a
   calendar chip — by menu and by `Alt+click`. In the explorer, confirm `Ctrl+click` still
   multi-selects.
8. **The right sidebar follows focus.** Split with Habits in one pane, start editing a habit, click
   the other pane and click back. Your half-finished edit should still be there.
9. **Restarting.** Hollow opens on Today, as before. Your tabs and your split are deliberately not
   restored.
10. **Look at it.** The tab strip, the seam between panes, the **Side | Stacked** control and the new
    right-click menus have not had a proper visual review yet. Anything cramped, misaligned or
    mismatched at Compact or Spacious density is worth reporting.

## Known issues

- **Tabs and the split are not remembered between runs.** Deliberate for now: reopening takes you to
  Today with a single tab.
- **Two panes at most**, side by side or stacked, always an even split. There is no draggable
  divider yet.
- The Planner's sub-tab (List / Calendar / Habits / Projects) is still session-only, so a restored
  Planner comes back on Calendar.
- **None of this is on mobile.** The mobile app keeps its own tab bar and is untouched.
- Right-clicking selected text that is *not* in an editor no longer offers Copy, because those
  surfaces now have their own menus. `Ctrl+C` still works everywhere.
